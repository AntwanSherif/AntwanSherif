# Voice & disclosure stance for the ADHD × coding-agents series

**Decision:** The ADHD × coding-agents series and talk are written **full first-person under my real name**, framing my ADHD as **self-identified, not clinically diagnosed**, stated transparently and early.

**Why:** Named vulnerability is what makes Post 1 escape the crowded "ADHD in engineering" lane and gives the talk its spine. Self-identification is honest *and* relatable — adult ADHD is heavily under-diagnosed (Lens A of the research brief), so a large share of the audience is self-identified too. Stating it upfront preempts credibility-policing ("you can't claim ADHD without a diagnosis").

**Guardrails:**
- Canonical phrasing: "self-identified with ADHD" / "I'm self-diagnosed" / "I recognize myself in the ADHD picture." Avoid bare "as someone with ADHD" where it implies a clinical stamp.
- Lived experience is narrated as mine; the science is cited to researchers.
- No clinical authority, no medical advice.

**Tone calibration (hireability) — added 2026-07-09:** This is the author's *first* published post and lives on a professional portfolio read by engineering managers. Write the ADHD brain **strength-forward, from mastery — not confession.** Keep the honesty and vulnerability (they're what make it land), but cut the signals that read as *unreliable employee*: can't-start-for-weeks, missed deadlines, self-neglect (forgetting to eat / vanishing for hours), "did nothing all month." Frame challenges as **tradeoffs actively managed into steady, shippable output**; lead with competence, and where it fits, bank a concrete delivered result *before* naming a cost. The net read should be: *a self-aware high performer who engineered a workflow around how he actually works.*
- **Applies series-wide.** Post 1 was written to this bar. Post 2 (crash beat — "too tired to read the replies", "built that graveyard more than once") and the Post 3 draft still need a light pass against it before publish.

**Trade-off accepted:** Permanent, search-indexed, career-visible disclosure under my real name — in exchange for authenticity and a differentiated speaking profile.
