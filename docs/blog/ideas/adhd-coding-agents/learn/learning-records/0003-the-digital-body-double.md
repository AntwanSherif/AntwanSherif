# 0003 — The digital body double

**Date:** 2026-07-01
**Lesson:** `lessons/0003-the-digital-body-double.html`

## What was taught
- Body doubling = doing a task in the presence of a person who is simply there — not helping, not supervising. The passive co-presence is the mechanism (Cleveland Clinic; Simply Psychology).
- Cleveland Clinic frames it as "external executive functioning" — "like having an administrative assistant follow you around all day."
- arXiv 2509.12153 (n=12, VR bricklaying): AI body double ≈ human body double; no significant efficiency difference (p=1.000). Both significantly outperformed working alone.
- **Honest-citation meta-skill:** direct trial evidence for body doubling is minimal — plausible/cheap/safe, not proven at scale. The defensible move is to say n=12 *first*: name the n → name the limit → name why it's still worth citing.
- Bridge: pairing with an agent ≈ a digital body double. Co-present, task-focused, patient — delivers the functional benefit without another human.

## Key insight
This is the lesson where evidence quality and citation skill fuse. The n=12 finding is the most vivid single datapoint in the whole deck *and* the easiest to misuse. The meta-skill — "say the small n before the skeptic does" — is exactly what the mission means by "handle a hostile Q&A without getting rattled." Teaching the formula explicitly (name the n / name the limit / name why it still matters) is the lesson's real payload.

## Open loops
- Body doubling's traditional evidence base (non-AI, non-VR) deserves a brief note — a few small studies + clinician consensus. If a skeptic asks "is body doubling itself proven?" the answer needs to be ready: plausible and widely practiced, not RCT-confirmed.
- The "twelve people on a bricklaying task" limitation is worth anticipating: does the task generalize to knowledge work? Honest answer: unknown — the study is a proof-of-concept, not an efficacy trial.
