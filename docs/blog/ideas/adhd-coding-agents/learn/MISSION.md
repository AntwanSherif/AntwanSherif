# Mission: The ADHD science behind "ADHD × coding agents"

## Why
I'm writing a 3-part blog series and a conference talk about being an engineer with (self-identified) ADHD who works daily with coding agents. I need to understand the underlying ADHD science well enough to defend every claim I make — live, on stage, in Q&A — without overclaiming or misstating a mechanism.

## Success looks like
- I can explain executive function, working memory, and context-switching cost in my own words, correctly, and name a source for each.
- I can handle a hostile Q&A question ("that stat is wrong" / "you can't claim ADHD without a diagnosis") without getting rattled — because I know the evidence and its limits.
- Every ADHD term in the posts is defined once, consistently, from a trusted source (the shared glossary), and I actually understand each definition rather than parroting it.
- I can tell the difference between what's peer-reviewed (prevalence, working-memory deficits, interruption cost) and what's practitioner framework or anecdote (interest-based nervous system, body-doubling efficacy) — and I say so out loud.

## Constraints
- Grounded in already-gathered sources: `../research-brief.md` and `../talks-and-community.md`. Never trust parametric knowledge — cite.
- Glossary must be authored as structured data (dual-use: this reference + an MDX `<Term>` component in the blog).
- Self-identified, not clinically diagnosed — so the framing must never imply clinical authority (see `../2026-07-01-01-voice-and-disclosure.md`).

## Out of scope
- ADHD treatment / medication science (not needed for the series; risks implying medical advice).
- Diagnostic criteria in clinical depth (DSM checklists) — beyond what's needed to honestly frame self-identification.
- Neuroscience deeper than mechanism-level (fMRI methodology, neurotransmitter pharmacology).
