The Interruption Tax

And the one number you must never say on stage.

Mission tie-in: you need to defend the "parallel agents overwhelm the ADHD brain" claim in live Q&A — without citing folklore. This lesson gives you the real evidence and the trap to avoid.

Start with the feeling, because the feeling is the whole talk:

"You think you are super productive … but you wake up the next day and see 10 terminals, 5 dead ssh sessions, 3 conversations you dont even remember about." — DEV Community, an ADHD developer · source

That is context-switching cost made visceral. Now here's what the research actually says — so you can turn the feeling into a fact.

What the evidence supports

In field studies of information workers, Gloria Mark and colleagues found people spent only ~11 minutes in a "working sphere" before switching or being interrupted.

The counterintuitive part: interrupted work was often completed faster, not slower — but at a measurable cost in stress, frustration, and mental workload. People compensate by working faster and writing less. (Cite the direction confidently; treat the exact p<.01 figure as needs-manual-verification — the PDF was inaccessible during research.)

⚠ Credibility landmine

The famous "23 minutes 15 seconds to refocus" figure has no traceable primary source. It appears only in informal interviews with Gloria Mark — never in a peer-reviewed paper. One investigator reviewed 5 papers and 23 blog posts and couldn't find it printed anywhere.

Do not say it. If someone in your audience knows the debunk, your whole talk's credibility takes the hit. Cite the real study instead.

The bridge to your thesis: running N agents in parallel is manufactured self-interruption. So the interruption research predicts exactly the double-edged sword — you finish faster and quietly burn your stress budget. That's not a vibe; it's the measured shape of interrupted work. The ADHD brain, already taxed on the same axis, just pays the toll sooner.
