Lesson 02 · Knowledge → Tactic

The Working-Memory Offload

Why "rename the sessions" is neuroscience, not tidiness.

Mission tie-in: your playbook's first tactic — externalizing state — needs to stand on evidence, not preference. This lesson turns a habit into a defensible claim.

Last lesson: parallel agents manufacture self-interruption, and the ADHD brain pays the stress toll early. This lesson: the first tactic that fights back — and it has a name in the literature.

Start, again, with a voice from the field — this time from the edge, not the pain:

"AI agents are external working memory. That's not a metaphor. That's literally what they do for me." — Chris Wright · source

What the evidence supports

Verified  A meta-analysis of 38 studies found moderate-magnitude working-memory deficits in adults with ADHD versus controls — across both phonological (verbal) and visuospatial domains. The deficits persist into adulthood; this isn't a childhood-only story.

Working memory is the small scratchpad that holds information "live" while you use it. Every open thread you keep in your head — which terminal does what, where you left off, what to check next — is spending a budget that's already short.

The mechanism: holding state in working memory costs energy. Executive function research calls the fix cognitive offloading: move the state out of your head and onto a durable surface, and you free the scratchpad for the actual thinking. This is a recognized executive-function intervention — not a productivity-blog trick.

◆ The tactic, grounded

So when you rename each agent session by its job, write the plan into a memory file, or let the agent's own context hold "where we left off" — you're not being tidy. You're externalizing working memory: putting the load on a surface that, unlike your scratchpad, doesn't leak.

The agent's superpower here is the mirror of your deficit: "It didn't get distracted. It didn't forget. It didn't move on to something more interesting. It just held the thought." That's the offload, working.
